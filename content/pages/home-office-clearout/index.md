My home office has reached the point where the equipment outnumbers the work being done on it. Some of it has been displaced by newer kit, some has simply been sitting in a drawer looking reproachful, and all of it still works. So it is going.

This is my own equipment. Nothing here is a reseller's stock, a review unit or a box of mystery returns. I have used every item on this page, I know its quirks, and I will tell you what they are before you find them yourself.

Every item includes free delivery, setup and initial support. If your setup needs something extra, such as a network switch or Wi-Fi access points, I will source it and add it at cost.

## What's on the table

### HP Spectre x360 13.5″ (2022): UGX 2,900,000

{{< screenshot-fig src="spectre-x360.webp" width="360" alt="HP Spectre x360 13.5 in Nocturne Blue" caption="Stock image. The Spectre, looking more presentable than most of us on a Monday." >}}

Core i7-1255U, 16 GB RAM, 1 TB SSD, and a 13.5″ 3:2 touchscreen on a 360° hinge. It has two Thunderbolt 4 ports, Wi-Fi 6E, a fingerprint reader and Windows Hello face login. It comes with the original HP stylus and a leather sleeve, and leaves on a clean Windows 11 Pro install.

The honest version: the 1255U is a U-series chip. It is an excellent all-day machine for documents, browsing, calls and light development, and a modest one for anything that renders. It has also been my daily driver, so it has done real work rather than sitting in a cabinet. It is in great condition regardless, which says more about HP's build quality than about my care.

Review: [Laptop Mag](https://laptopmag.com/reviews/hp-spectre-x360-14-2022-review)

### Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 4: UGX 2,200,000

{{< screenshot-fig src="galaxy-fold-4.webp" width="360" alt="Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 4 in Phantom Black, partly unfolded" caption="Stock image. Mine has a fresh inner screen; the crease came free of charge." >}}

Snapdragon 8+ Gen 1, 12 GB RAM, 256 GB storage, and a 7.6″ inner screen with a 6.2″ cover screen, both at 120 Hz. It has a 50 MP main camera with ultra-wide and 3× telephoto, IPX8, 5G, wireless charging and S Pen Fold Edition support.

The inner screen is fresh and the hinge is smooth. The crease is still there, as it is on every Fold ever made. You stop seeing it within a week, and then you can't use a normal phone without feeling slightly cheated. It comes with two Spigen screen protectors and Spigen camera protection.

Review: [GSMArena](https://www.gsmarena.com/samsung_galaxy_z_fold4-review-2468.php)

### Firewalla Gold SE: UGX 900,000

{{< screenshot-fig src="firewalla-gold-se.webp" width="360" alt="Firewalla Gold SE showing its two 2.5G and two 1G Ethernet ports" caption="Stock image. Small box, large opinions about your network." >}}

A router and firewall with no subscription fees. It has 2 × 2.5 GbE and 2 × 1 GbE ports, IDS/IPS, ad and tracker blocking, WireGuard and OpenVPN, multi-WAN failover, VLANs and per-device flow monitoring.

This is the one item on the page that can bite. It is very capable, and its app makes powerful things look easy right up until they aren't. That is why it does not leave without being configured for your network: router or bridge mode, VLANs, VPN and blocking rules, done properly. You get a working network, not a box and a QR code.

Review: [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2166704/firewalla-gold-se-review-superb-network-protection-no-subscription-required.html)

### Samsung Galaxy Watch 4 (40 mm): UGX 300,000

{{< screenshot-fig src="galaxy-watch-4.webp" width="360" alt="Samsung Galaxy Watch 4 in black" caption="Stock image. Mine comes armoured: case and screen protector included." >}}

Wear OS with Google Play. It tracks ECG, body composition, heart rate, SpO2 and sleep, and is rated 5ATM + IP68. It comes with a screen protector, a protective case and spare Spigen accessories.

One caveat: ECG only works when paired with a Samsung phone. Pair it with anything else and it is still a very good smartwatch, just a less clinical one.

Review: [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/samsung-galaxy-watch-4-review)

### 2 × Dell E2020H 19.5″ monitors: UGX 280,000 each

{{< screenshot-fig src="dell-e2020h.webp" width="360" alt="Dell E2020H 19.5-inch monitor" caption="Stock image of one E2020H. You get two. Not glamorous; never once late for work." >}}

1600 × 900, anti-glare, DisplayPort and VGA inputs, flicker-free with ComfortView, and VESA 100 compatible.

These are TN panels and nobody has ever wept at their colour accuracy. They are, however, exactly what second and third screens should be: present, reliable and uncomplaining. If you're pairing them with the Spectre, you'll need two USB-C-to-DisplayPort cables, as the laptop has no DisplayPort output of its own.

Specs: [B&H](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1579475-REG/dell_e2020h_20_led_lcd_monitor.html/overview)

### Dual monitor desk stand: UGX 150,000

{{< screenshot-fig src="monitor-stand.webp" width="360" alt="Dual monitor arm stand with weighted base" caption="Stock image. Shown holding larger Dell monitors; the stand is for sale, those monitors aren't." >}}

It holds both monitors side by side on VESA mounts and gives you back the desk space their own stands were occupying. There is not much else to say about a stand, other than that this one has never dropped anything.

### HP LaserJet Pro P1102: UGX 350,000

{{< screenshot-fig src="hp-p1102.webp" width="360" alt="HP LaserJet Pro P1102 mono laser printer" caption="Stock image. A toaster that prints." >}}

Mono laser, 18 ppm, USB, using standard HP 85A toner. It has one full cartridge installed and one spare that has never been opened.

Every review of this printer complains that the toner is expensive. That's fair, and it's also why the second cartridge matters: you won't be buying toner for a good while.

Review: [Cartridge Save](https://cartridgesave.co.uk/printwhatmatters/tried-and-tested/hp-laserjet-pro-p1102)
